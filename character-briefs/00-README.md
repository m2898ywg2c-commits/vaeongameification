# The eight character briefs

One file per character, because Canva chokes on a long document and because eight characters in
one file is how a rule gets changed for one of them and not the other seven.

Each brief is **standalone**. It carries its own prompt, its own palette, its own layer
filenames and the locked rules repeated in full. Hand over one file, get back one character.
Everything the briefs share, they repeat rather than reference: a brief that says "see the
other document for the rules" is a brief whose rules do not get read.

`../CHARACTER-BRIEF.md` holds what is true of the whole set and not needed to draw any single
one of them: why the shipped robots have to be remade, the rig and pivot maths, the three-beat
completion fanfare, and where Canva does and does not fit.

The numbering is the order to make them in, not a ranking. **The Hunter is first because it is
the pilot**: strongest colour in the set, the type James holds so he will spot a wrong note
fastest, and the one currently getting its identity entirely from its background, which makes
it the hardest case rather than the easiest. Take it all the way through to layered files and a
working animation in the app before anybody generates a second character.

## Two pairs to watch

Both are flagged again inside their own briefs, but they are set-level problems and this is
where they live.

**The Captain and the Monk are 20 degrees apart in hue**, measured on the rendered artwork
rather than the hex codes, and they are the tightest pair in the set. That is why they are
consecutive: make them back to back and put them side by side at 38px before committing to
anything after them. The Captain is the most built-up figure in the set and the Monk is the
smoothest, and that contrast is what has to carry them apart, because the colours barely will.

**The Spark and the Hunter** are both warm, coral against orange. Round against trim, and the
largest visor in the set against the smallest, is all that separates them.

## They fit Canva's 4,000 character limit

Every brief is between 3,500 and 3,800 characters, so one file pastes into a Canva field
whole. That is what set the length: they were 4,600 to 4,900 and had to come down.

If you hit a shorter field, **paste the prompt block on its own**. It is about 950 characters
and it carries the palette, the pose, the camera and the transparent background, which is
everything the image generator actually acts on. The rest of the brief is for the human
judging the result, and no generator has ever read a markdown table.

## What every brief asks for, in one line

Seven layers on an identical 2048px canvas, genuinely transparent, glow drawn in flat white so
the app tints it with the user's own accent, one shared neutral pose so all eight can share one
animation rig. If a delivery misses any of those four, it is a picture rather than an asset and
we are back where we started.
